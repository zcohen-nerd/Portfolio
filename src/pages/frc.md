# *FIRST* Robotics Competition

I've been part of the *FIRST* Robotics Competition for two decades — first as a founding student on a team, then as a mentor, lead mentor, judge, and event manager. What started as building a robot in high school became a throughline for how I approach engineering: complex systems succeed when the people, processes, and technology are designed together.

Today my focus is delivering competitions for *FIRST* Chesapeake — coordinating the referees, judges, inspectors, field crews, and volunteer staffs that make a three-day event with hundreds of students run on schedule. For the 2027 season I will serve as Event Manager of the Central Maryland District Event at South River High School and as Event Manager of the *FIRST* Chesapeake District Championship at Showplace Arena — the region's culminating event.

## Engineering and systems leadership

A district competition is a system of systems: ~36 teams, 700–1,500 students, 100+ volunteers, and three days of matches that have to stay on schedule. As Event Manager I coordinate the specialized volunteer groups — refereeing, judging, inspection, field operations, pit administration — and make sure their work integrates into one reliable operation.

That is the same job systems engineering does on a hardware program: define the interfaces, trust the specialists, watch the critical path, and manage escalations before small failures ripple outward. Event leadership taught me that discipline years before I applied it to autonomous platforms.

## Teaching and mentorship

As Lead Mentor of the Power Hawks (2013–2017), I focused on building an engineering organization rather than coaching a robot build. The program grew from roughly 36 FRC students to more than 60, added three FTC teams, and ran with a staff of 30+ professional mentors each season. Students worked in functional subteams with real ownership; my job was structure, leadership development, and keeping the program aligned with its mission.

The outcomes are the point: more than 500 students came through the program in that period, roughly 80% pursued STEM degrees, and 98% went on to four-year universities. Competitively, those seasons brought the team's first event win (2017), two finalist appearances, three World Championship qualifications, and Chairman's and Engineering Inspiration awards at both regional and district levels.

As a judge — primarily a Team Attributes Judge from district events up through the World Championship — I evaluate the same things I tried to build as a mentor: student ownership, engineering culture, and the structures that let teams sustain themselves beyond one graduating class.

## Programs and advocacy

During the 2015–2016 season the Power Hawks took the mission beyond the field. Working with Maryland Delegate Eric Reznik as the effort's primary point of contact, I coordinated a student-driven push to introduce state legislation supporting after-school robotics — which passed, providing **$250,000 annually** for programs across Maryland. Each year I also led students to Capitol Hill to advocate for STEM education directly with members of Congress.

In 2016 the team hosted a *FIRST* Chesapeake district event at our school — and deliberately sat out the competition so students and mentors could staff and run it, seeing the operational side of the program firsthand.

## What *FIRST* taught me

- **Engineering is a team sport.** The best teams operate like small engineering organizations — success is integration across specialties, not a lone expert.
- **Leadership means enabling others.** Whether coordinating mentors or 100+ event volunteers, the job is building the environment where experts do their best work.
- **Systems thinking matters.** Small failures ripple; stable systems come from understanding how components interact.
- **Culture drives outcomes.** Teams with ownership and curiosity out-build teams with better parts.

These lessons carried directly into how I design hardware platforms like [SENTRY](/projects/sentry-v3/), how I [teach engineering](/teaching/), and how I run integration on professional autonomous systems.
